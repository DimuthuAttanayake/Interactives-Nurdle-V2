Plastic Pollution

The life of a single nurdle.

How a five-millimetre plastic pellet travels from the refinery to the far corners of the ocean — and never really leaves.

Meet the nurdle

Plastics as we know them — from water bottles to cars — are created from small, translucent plastic polymers called “nurdles.”

How small?

These are approximately 5 mm in size — about the size of a lentil.

Where they are born

Plastics are a byproduct of oil and gas production — leftover chemicals from refining the oil and gas are used to manufacture nurdles.

Transport · routine loss

Some nurdles are lost to the environment during routine handling.

By rail

The rest are transported away by train…

By sea

…or by ships.

The spills

They’re released through routine handling failures and accidents along the supply chain — a lesser-known but pervasive form of microplastic pollution.

Rail derailment

In rail accidents, spilled nurdles end up in the water systems.

Shipping accident

Nurdle spills are now the hallmark of modern ship disasters.

Worldwide

The spills add up to a pollution problem without borders.

Into the water

Most of the nurdles lost from ships end up in rivers, oceans and shores around the world…

Microplastics

…further disintegrating into smaller plastics…

Persisting

…and surviving beyond a human lifespan.

The nurdle’s journey

One pellet — and almost none of it ever goes away.

Every nurdle that escapes outlasts the people who made it, breaking into ever-smaller plastic that lingers in the water for generations.
